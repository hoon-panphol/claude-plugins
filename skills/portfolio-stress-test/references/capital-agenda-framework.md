# Capital Agenda Framework — adapted for a personal portfolio

Source concept: *The Stress Test Every Business Needs: A Capital Agenda for Confidently
Facing Recessions, Digital Disruption, Difficult Investors, and Geopolitical Threats*
(Greene, Krouskos, Hood, Basnayake, Casey — EY / Wiley).

The book extends the bank-regulatory "stress test" idea to a company's **Capital Agenda**:
how an organization Raises, Invests, Optimizes, and Preserves capital — and argues that
resilience has to be built into each of those four activities *and* into how they interact,
against a broad definition of stress: macroeconomic/recession risk, geopolitical risk,
digital/technological disruption, and difficult investors (activist shareholders, hostile
takeovers).

This skill treats a personal investment portfolio as a miniature version of that same
Capital Agenda. The four corporate activities map onto four portfolio-level questions:

| Book's Capital Agenda | Corporate meaning | Portfolio meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Raising** | Ability to access funding/capital markets when needed, on good terms | Liquidity: can this holding be sold/rebalanced quickly, near fair value, without forced-seller losses? (bid-ask spread, fund gates/lock-ups, market depth, redemption terms) |
| **Investing** | Deploying capital into the right strategic bets | Valuation vs. fundamentals: is the holding priced sensibly against its current earnings/macro outlook, or is it running ahead of (or lagging) the fundamentals? |
| **Optimizing** | Efficiency — capital structure, cost of capital, portfolio of businesses | Efficiency: cost drag (expense ratio, FX drag, tax drag), and diversification value (does it reduce or add to overall portfolio correlation/concentration?) |
| **Preserving** | Protecting the balance sheet against shocks | Downside protection: historical drawdown behavior, credit/balance-sheet quality, dividend/coupon reliability, defensiveness in the specific shocks below |

## Scoring rubric (apply per holding — asset class or individual stock)

Score each of the four dimensions **1 (fragile) to 5 (resilient)**. Do not default to the
middle — use the research to actually differentiate. Write one sentence of justification per
score so the dashboard can show *why*.

- **Raising (Liquidity) 1–5**
  - 1: illiquid, wide spreads, gated fund, thin market cap / low daily volume
  - 3: normal liquidity for its asset class
  - 5: deep, liquid market even under stress (e.g., large-cap developed equity, on-the-run
    sovereign bonds, gold)

- **Investing (Valuation vs. Fundamentals) 1–5**
  - 1: priced well above what current earnings/macro outlook supports, or deteriorating
    fundamentals not yet reflected in price
  - 3: roughly fairly valued given consensus outlook
  - 5: cheap relative to quality/fundamentals, or fundamentals improving faster than price

- **Optimizing (Efficiency & Diversification fit) 1–5**
  - 1: high cost (expense ratio/spread), highly correlated with the rest of the portfolio,
    adds concentration
  - 3: reasonable cost, moderate correlation
  - 5: low cost, genuinely diversifying (low/negative correlation to the largest holdings)

- **Preserving (Downside protection) 1–5**
  - 1: history of deep drawdowns, weak balance sheet/credit quality, dividend/coupon at risk
  - 3: average resilience for its category
  - 5: defensive history, strong balance sheet, reliable income, tends to hold up or hedge in
    downturns (e.g., quality bonds, gold, staples with pricing power)

## Additional per-holding stress flags (from the book's broader stress definition)

Beyond the 4-dimension score, flag each holding (stock-level especially) for these
book-specific stress categories — yes/no + one-line note:

- **Recession exposure** — cyclicality of revenue/earnings to GDP/consumer spending
- **Digital disruption exposure** — is the business model or sub-sector being disrupted by
  new technology/competitors (book Ch. "Can your strategy thrive in a digital world?")
- **Difficult investors / governance exposure** — active activist campaigns, hostile
  takeover chatter, weak governance, concentrated insider selling, key-person risk
- **Geopolitical exposure** — sanctions, tariff exposure, single-country/regulatory
  concentration, supply-chain choke points

For asset classes (rather than single stocks), translate these to the asset-class level,
e.g. "EM equities → geopolitical exposure: high (trade policy, currency risk)."

## Aggregating to portfolio level

- Portfolio dimension score = weighted average of holding scores by portfolio weight (%
  allocated), one weighted score per dimension (Raising/Investing/Optimizing/Preserving).
- Call out the 2-3 lowest-scoring holdings by weight — these drive the overall grade more
  than small positions.
- Note where a **rebalance-band breach** and a **low Preserving score** overlap — that's the
  combination the book flags as compounding risk (a position that's both oversized and
  fragile).
